_CLS / Mainz, 2010–2019_

The nucleon axial charge g<sub>A</sub> is known precisely from neutron β-decay and is therefore a benchmark for lattice calculations of nucleon structure. At the time of our calculation, many lattice determinations were systematically below the experimental value. We showed that contributions from excited states are a significant source of systematic error at the source–sink separations used at the time, and that the summation method allows them to be controlled {% cite Capitani:2012gj %}.

We subsequently applied the same strategy to the electromagnetic and axial form factors of the nucleon {% cite Capitani:2015sba Capitani:2017qpc %}. In addition, I worked on improved interpolating operators for hadrons at non-zero momentum and on the shape of smeared sources {% cite DellaMorte:2012xc vonHippel:2013yfa %}. The explicit treatment of excited states is now a standard part of nucleon-structure calculations.

**Contribution.** Computation of nucleon two- and three-point functions, smeared sources and interpolating fields at non-zero momentum, and the excited-state analysis.

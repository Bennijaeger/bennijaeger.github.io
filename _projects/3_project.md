*FASTSUM, 2013–today*

Within the FASTSUM collaboration, we study hadrons across the transition to the quark–gluon plasma using anisotropic lattices with N<sub>f</sub> = 2+1 flavours of Wilson quarks. The small temporal lattice spacing provides more Euclidean-time points at a given temperature than an isotropic lattice.

We have shown that the positive- and negative-parity partners of the nucleon and of strange baryons, which have different masses at low temperature, become approximately degenerate above the transition {% cite Aarts:2015mma Aarts:2017rrl Aarts:2018glk %}. This parity doubling is a direct signal of chiral symmetry restoration in the baryonic sector. In a related project with the Mainz group, we studied charge transport and vector-meson dissociation in two-flavour QCD {% cite Brandt:2015aqk %}.

With the Gen2L ensembles (m<sub>π</sub> ≈ 239 MeV), we determined properties of the thermal transition {% cite Aarts:2020vyb %}, open-charm mesons and charmed baryons {% cite Aarts:2022krz Aarts:2023nax %}, the curvature of the pseudocritical line {% cite Smecca:2024gpu %} and the effective restoration of U(1)<sub>A</sub> symmetry {% cite Aarts:2026kpq %}.

**Contribution.** Generation of gauge configurations and correlation functions; baryon correlators on the Gen2 ensembles and their analysis; since 2023, the computing applications as PI of several EuroHPC and DeiC allocations.

**Outlook.** The main limitation of these results is that the pion mass is above its physical value and that only one temporal lattice spacing is available at the lighter pion mass. Therefore, we cannot yet separate the light-quark-mass dependence from temporal discretisation effects. The next generation of ensembles (Gen2P, Gen3L and Gen3P) is designed to remove these two limitations. Note that the spatial lattice spacing is approximately the same for all ensembles, so that this programme does not yet allow a continuum extrapolation.

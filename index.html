I am Associate Professor in Computational Quantum Field Theory at the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science (IMADA) of the University of Southern Denmark (SDU) in Odense, and Senior Fellow of the Danish Institute for Advanced Study (DIAS).

My research is in lattice quantum field theory, i.e. the computation of QCD and related theories from first principles by large-scale numerical simulations. These calculations combine quantum field theory with applied mathematics: Markov-chain Monte Carlo methods, stochastic differential equations, statistical inference and inverse problems. They are only feasible on large high-performance-computing systems. Most of this work is carried out in international collaborations, in particular FASTSUM, and with the lattice group in Odense.

Current topics are

  • QCD at non-zero temperature. Within FASTSUM, we study hadrons across the transition to the quark–gluon plasma on anisotropic lattices: parity doubling of baryons, open charm, the curvature of the pseudocritical line and the effective restoration of U(1)A symmetry.
  • Spectral reconstruction. The extraction of spectral functions from Euclidean correlators is an ill-posed inverse problem. We compare maximum-likelihood, Backus–Gilbert and kernel-ridge-regression methods on the same data.
  • The sign problem. Complex Langevin simulations of QCD at non-zero baryon density, including the dynamical stabilisation method.
  • One-flavour QCD at large Nc as a numerical approach to the bosonic sector of 𝒩 = 1 super Yang–Mills theory.

Earlier, during my PhD in Mainz, I worked on the hadronic contribution to the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon and on the axial charge of the nucleon. The research page summarises each topic and my contribution, and the publications page lists all works.

The simulations rely on competitive computing allocations. As PI or Co-PI, I have contributed to allocations of approximately 1.8 billion CPU core-hours and 3.3 million GPU core-hours on national and European systems (EuroHPC, PRACE, STFC DiRAC, DeiC, LUMI).

At SDU, I teach programming, statistics for data science, computational physics and high-performance computing, and I am Degree Responsible for Mathematics–Economics. Details are on the teaching page.

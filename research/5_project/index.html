Spectral reconstruction

An ill-posed inverse problem in thermal QCD

FASTSUM, 2019–today

Spectral functions are related to Euclidean correlators by an integral transform. Their extraction from a finite number of statistically uncertain data points is an ill-posed inverse problem, and different methods can give different answers for the same data.

Within FASTSUM, we applied and compared maximum-likelihood, Backus–Gilbert and kernel-ridge-regression methods for bottomonium in thermal QCD (Offler et al., 2022; Page et al., 2022; Spriggs et al., 2022; Spriggs et al., 2022; Lombardo et al., 2025). The comparison shows that the methods agree reasonably well for ground-state masses, but differ for the widths, in particular at high temperature, where fewer time slices are available.

Ideally, one would like a method with a reliable estimate of its own systematic uncertainty. Currently, the most robust approach is to test each method on mock data with the same temporal extent and noise level as the lattice data.

Contribution. Application of machine-learning methods (kernel ridge regression) and the systematic comparison of methods.
